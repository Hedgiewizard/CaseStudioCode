Gateway Arch National Park App

Designer: Grace Lewis

Problem Statement

How would you make an app for a national park that people would want to use?

Objectives & Goals

My Objectives

My objective with this project was to design a usable app for a national park. That means I want it to be easy to navigate and straightforward so it is easy to find the exact information you are looking for.

Audience Objectives

A navigable app that lets you find information and get the most out of your trip.

Business Objectives

Make an app that gets people excited to visit the Gateway Arch and gets them to book tickets to visit.

Product Users & Changes

Who is using this site?

People planning a trip to the Gateway Arch. Families with teen aged kids.

Product Users

My product users were my parents. I asked them because they are both people who work in product design for a living. They helped me look at it more from a business point of view and make sure I had all the best practice things that I might have forgotten. They were very helpful.

User Needs

  • Easily able to find information about the attractions
  • Easily keep track of what they have planned
  • Easily get around

Features & Functionalities

I am trying to make the app as straightforward as possible.

User Challenges

  • At first I didn’t have back buttons on the “calendar” and “picking a time” my dad said I should probably add those
  • There were some problems with the original ways I worded some of the things
  • Labeling icons to be more clear of what they mean. At first, my parents didn’t understand all the connections
  • The landing page used to be way too content-heavy. It wasn’t clear that it was supposed to be a landing page
  • There were some problems with the original ways I worded some of the things
  • The wording in the checkout process wasn’t entirely consistent and my mom said that would probably end up confusing people
  • Wording with “visit” instead of “trip”
  • Labeling months to make it clear that it is a calendar
  • My mom’s exact words were “You need to design to the greatest idiot”. Which honestly is good advice when designing and coding.
  • I had to change some button colors to fit better with the scheme I had going on Like originally the discount button was the same green as the ‘moving on’ buttons and my dad said that could possibly get confusing because “you’ve conditioned me(the user) to correlate this color with moving forward”
  • Needing a clearer way to go to checkout. I originally didn’t have a clear button for that
  • On the “experiences” page I originally had “book” instead of “get tickets”. It was pointed out that the difference in wording would probably lead to confusion
  • I was also advised to change the wording of ‘cart’ and ‘checkout’ because I had set the standard of giving directions in the first page
  • Adding a back button next to submit order
  • Changing ‘confirm your trip’ to ‘reservation confirmation’ because, “you already bought them. Saying you are confirming it implies you haven’t paid yet.”

Competitor Analysis

The ‘competitor’ we are using here is ‘gatewayarch.com’ which to my knowledge is the official gateway arch site.

Things that are liked

  • I like their colors, but that is more of a designer thing
  • I like how they put little icons next to places under the experience tab
  • It’s pretty straightforward
  • They present the information in a way that doesn’t feel overwhelming

Things that are disliked

  • Things are a little spread out. Which isn’t my favorite. But that might be a me thing
  • It loops in circles sometimes and can be difficult to find clear information.

Notable Changes

  • I tried to give times as often as I could. A lot of other apps and sights I’ve seen make it unreasonably difficult to find what times things are open.
  • I tried to make needed information as easy to find as possible
